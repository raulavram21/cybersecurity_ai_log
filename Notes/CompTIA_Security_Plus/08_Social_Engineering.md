# Social Engineering

## Overview

Social engineering uses tactics that manipulate people into revealing information or taking actions they would not normally take. It is one of the most common attacks because it is low effort for the attacker and often effective.

## Phishing Variants

| Type | Channel | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Phishing** | Email | Mass messages that impersonate a trusted source |
| **Spear phishing** | Email | Targeted messages aimed at a specific person |
| **Whaling** | Email | Spear phishing aimed at senior executives such as the CEO |
| **Vishing** | Voice calls | Phone calls that impersonate someone trusted |
| **Smishing** | SMS and text | Fraudulent text messages with links or requests |

> **Analogy:** Phishing is casting a wide net. Spear phishing is a diver with a spear gun, choosing one particular fish. Whaling is going after the biggest fish in the sea.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Exam clue:** If a question mentions the CEO, it is very likely asking about whaling.

## Misinformation and Disinformation

- **Misinformation:** False information spread without intent to deceive
- **Disinformation:** False information spread deliberately to persuade or create a belief that something is wrong when it may not be

> **Analogy:** Misinformation is a rumour passed on in good faith. Disinformation is the same rumour, planted on purpose to cause panic.

## Pretexting

Pretexting is creating a believable scenario, or pretext, that makes a target comfortable enough to share information or act. The attacker does not describe it as an attack.

> **Analogy:** A person in a hi-vis vest claiming to be a utility engineer so you let them into the building.

## Watering Hole Attack

A watering hole attack compromises a website that a target group is known to visit, rather than attacking the target directly. The attacker waits for the victims to come to them.

> **Analogy:** Poisoning the water at the single well where a group of animals drinks.
