# Threat Actors

## Overview

Threat actors are the people or groups behind attacks. Each has a different skill level, motivation, and level of access, which shapes the threats an organization must plan for.

## Threat Actor Profiles

| Threat actor | Sophistication | Motivation | Resources | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nation-state (APT)** | High | National interests, espionage, disruption, war | High | External |
| **Organized crime** | High | Money | High | External |
| **Hacktivist** | Moderate | Disruption, ethics or philosophy, politics | Moderate | External |
| **Insider threat** | Moderate | Disruption, revenge, blackmail | Low | Internal |
| **Unskilled attacker** | Low | Disruption, prestige | Low | External |
| **Shadow IT** | Low to moderate | Prestige | Low | Internal |

## Nation-State (APT)

Nation-state actors are government-backed attackers, driven by national interests rather than profit. They have high skill and high resources.

> **Analogy:** A well-funded intelligence agency running a long campaign, where the goal is information or influence, not quick cash.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **APT on the exam:** The term advanced persistent threat (APT) is reserved for nation-state attackers. In the industry, organized crime groups are also sometimes called APTs.

## Organized Crime

Criminal groups with high skill and resources. They are only motivated by money and do not care about political messaging.

> **Analogy:** A professional heist crew. The target is the payout, and the method is whatever works best.

## Hacktivist

Attackers with a cause, such as supporting a political position, a foreign country, or an ethical principle. They have moderate skill and resources.

> **Analogy:** A protest that uses digital tools instead of placards, where the attack is the message.

## Insider Threat

An attacker who operates from inside the organization, with internal access but low resources. Motivations include disruption, revenge, and blackmail.

> **Analogy:** A locksmith who already works in the building and holds a spare key.

> [!WARNING]
> **Unintentional insiders:** An uneducated employee can also cause harm. They do not mean to, but the damage is still real.

## Unskilled Attacker

Low skill and low resources, often called a script kiddie in older terminology. Motivated by disruption or prestige, and they often use existing tools without understanding them.

> **Analogy:** Someone who follows a prepared recipe without knowing how the ingredients work.

## Shadow IT

An internal employee with some technical knowledge who tries to help by working around official processes. Motivated by prestige, with low resources.

> **Analogy:** A helpful colleague who sets up their own server because the official IT process feels too slow, and creates risk without meaning to.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Insider threat vs shadow IT:** Both have internal access. The insider threat is hostile, while shadow IT acts with good intent but still creates unmanaged risk.
